      How well does the classifier transfer?

      On 2,060 held-out microscope images from four collections, the original classifier’s first choice matched the expert-mapped label on 6 images (0.3%). Its most frequent mistake was calling an organism a bubble or wave, which it did on 1,482 images.

      The optional source-specific ranking learned from other acquisitions in those four collections. Given the collection name, it matched 518 of the same 2,060 images (25.1%). On two collections held out in full, it used the original ranking, which matched 28 of 1,194 images. Together, the six collections covered 28 of the 101 categories. A separate Tara Pacific test covered three additional taxonomic categories and one historical label.

      Can the classifier distinguish bubbles from larvae in VPR images?

      We tested a separate research classifier on Video Plankton Recorder images from two cruises held out from training. It detected 146 of 220 Arctic bubbles, up from 130, with gains varying by acquisition. On a Canadian cruise, it kept 241 of 250 larvae out of the bubble category, up from 64. It labeled all 14 Arctic amphipods “other organism.”

On a fresh Japanese cruise, false bubble calls fell from 31 to 28 of 222 organisms. The cruise had no expert-confirmed bubbles for measuring recall.

      Recovering six Arctic Themisto

      After training on the 6 known Themisto, the model recognized all 6 as amphipods. When each animal was held out in turn, it recognized 4 of 6. On a separate Greenland–Labrador cruise, the previous and retrained models each called 6 of 300 calanoids and 0 of 128 ostracods amphipods. The retrained model recognized 20 of 22 expert-labeled hyperiids there.

      On a new Greenland Sea leg, the retrained model recognized 57 of 63 genus-confirmed Themisto, up from 56. Training without the 6 known animals recovered the same additional specimen. Across 1,623 new images, neither retrained model introduced a calanoid or ostracod error. The decision rule kept all prior ostracod classifications.

      Calanoid lookalikes on a fresh Arctic cruise

      The retrained model corrected the known PS126 calanoid error and still recognized both previously recovered Themisto. On a new Barents–Kara cruise, it called 6 of 360 calanoids amphipods, down from 126. Both models misclassified 1 of 107 ostracods as an amphipod. The retrained model recognized 44 of 46 expert-labeled Arctic hyperiids. On earlier cruises it missed 6 Themisto the previous model had recognized.

      Read the paired cruise results and specimen review

      Themisto with faint appendages

      The retrained model still missed both Fram Strait Themisto. An alternative cutoff recovered both on replay and also called one calanoid an amphipod. On new Nordic and Iroise Sea scans, that cutoff added no errors among 199 expert-labeled ostracods. It recognized 100 of 120 broader hyperiids and all 3 genus-confirmed Themisto.

      Read the matched review and geographic test

      Inherited ZooScan ostracod errors

      On two newly reserved Arctic cruises, the retrained model eliminated all 55 false amphipod calls among 310 expert-labeled ostracods. The model recognized 188 of 239 Themisto, compared with 190 before retraining. It preserved all 21 scanner-added detections entering this retrain. The two losses were animals the original classifier also recognized.

      ZooScan ostracods and Arctic amphipods

      On two newly reserved Fram Strait cruises, retraining removed 106 ostracod false amphipod calls and retained 2 of the 3 Themisto detections added by the earlier ZooScan model.

      Arctic amphipods by instrument

      The VPR models recognized 1 of 6 expert-confirmed Themisto frames on two Healy acquisitions and kept 49 of 50 bubble detections. On two new Arctic cruises, a model trained for ZooScan recognized 75 additional Themisto and made 20 additional false amphipod calls on controls.
