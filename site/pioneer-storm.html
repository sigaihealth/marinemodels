The observations

January 29 – February 3, 2026

What the mooring saw

A microscope at 7 m captured 123,609 image regions in 42 water samples. A region can show a plankton cell, a chain, or suspended debris.

On February 1 the buoy recorded a pressure minimum of 995.02 mbar at 08:34 UTC. Practical salinity fell from 35.061 to 34.582 between the measurements nearest the two pictured samples.

Reading the measurements

OOI flags the particle concentrations and pressure minimum as unevaluated by automated quality control. The matched salinity readings passed. The cause of the particle increase remains open.

Plankton recognition

What’s in
the images?

We ran Woods Hole’s Northeast Shelf classifier on 24 microscope crops. Its most frequent first choice was “mixed nanoplankton.” Detritus came second.

The gallery contains 12 examples from each of two samples. These model suggestions await expert review. Estimating how much plankton was present requires an independent audit across the event.

The 101-category SIG.AI Plankton Classifier used with our PlankScope instrument also returned predictions for all 24 original crops. Both models saw the same images. The planned blind review will test how well their identifications carry across camera types using broad groups that experts can distinguish.

Research roadmap

Toward a coastal
digital twin

SIG.AI Ocean is working toward an updateable model of this coastal site. The next experiments will test which observations can explain past changes and predict the next sample.

Audit the images

Draw 420 crops at random, ten from each sample. Expert reviewers will label them blind so we can estimate errors and report concentrations for groups they can distinguish.

Explain the evidence

Benchmark a 35-billion-parameter open-weight model, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, against an OpenAI model. Score reports on numbers, citations, and whether they acknowledge missing evidence.

Test a forecast

Use the longer moored record to find comparable storms and test six-hour forecasts on held-out events. Recovered images can support retrospective comparisons.
