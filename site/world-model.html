    From observations to a forecast

    Start with one testable question. Can we predict storm-driven changes in temperature and salinity at Pioneer?

    1. Estimate the present

      Align temperature, salinity and wind records by location, depth, timestamp and quality flag. Keep a record of what was available when the forecast was issued.

    2. Advance the state

      Use recent ocean conditions and wind forecasts to predict station temperature and salinity 6, 24 and 72 hours ahead, with an uncertainty interval at each lead.

    3. Test against the ocean

      Withhold whole storms. Compare errors against carrying the last observation forward and check how often observations fall inside each prediction interval.

    Biological extension · Test whether adding earlier plankton imagery improves forecasts. The agent can retrieve supporting frames, measurements and model outputs for review.

    Winter Storm Gianna at Pioneer

    Off North Carolina, an Imaging FlowCytobot sampled water at 7 m as the storm passed. The mooring recorded 42 samples over six days. Each dot represents one sample time.

    Imaged particles

    The buoy recorded its lowest pressure, 995.02 mbar, at 08:34 UTC on February 1. The microscope’s image regions can contain individual cells, chains, or debris.
