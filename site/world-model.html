    From observations to a forecast

    How accurately can the latest mooring, buoy and satellite observations forecast a change at Pioneer?

    Six-hour Pioneer storm forecast

    Carrying the last mooring reading forward gave the lowest average error for both targets. We tested 926 hourly forecasts across 14 later storms and weighted each storm equally.

    Mean absolute error at six hours · lower is better

    Winds and waves helped the history model forecast temperature on 11 of 14 storms. Adding the archived satellite sea-surface temperature maps increased average error for both targets. The median map was 9.5 days old at issue time.

    Tide and currents at six hours

    Two completed storms had usable mooring observations that the earlier benchmark had never used in training or scoring. Adding tide and currents reduced salinity error in both storms. Currents helped temperature in September and increased its error in April.

    Mean absolute error · two completed storms weighted equally · 75 forecasts
    ForecastTemperature (°C)Practical salinity

    Tide0.3390.268

    In the first 20 six-hour forecasts verified for the November 2026 storm, tide plus currents gave the lowest temperature error (0.796 °C versus 0.946 °C for persistence). Tide alone gave the lowest salinity error (0.426 versus 0.451). The four-storm scorecard tracks salinity across the full November event and the next three storms using the fixed tide-and-current model.

    1. Estimate the present

      Align temperature, salinity and wind records by location, depth, timestamp and quality flag. Keep a record of what was available when the forecast was issued.

    2. Advance the state

      Build on the six-hour test by evaluating 24- and 72-hour predictions on new storms, with uncertainty intervals at each lead.

    3. Test against the ocean

      Withhold whole storms. Compare errors against carrying the last observation forward and check how often observations fall inside each prediction interval.

    Animals on the seafloor

    On February 19, 2020, MBARI’s Ventana recorded a 20-second benthic transect. Researchers annotated 94 animals across 600 frames. This Pacific record comes from a different site and year than the Pioneer storm.
