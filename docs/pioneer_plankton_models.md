# Which particles increased during Winter Storm Gianna?

The Pioneer replay shows a 7.45-fold rise in image regions per mL between two February 1 samples as the water freshened. A first modeling project can ask how much of that change was associated with diatoms, other plankton, detritus, or material that the instrument cannot resolve. An image region can contain a single organism, a chain, or debris. [OOI event report](https://oceanobservatories.org/2026/09/ooi-plankton-imagery-and-co-located-environmental-data-now-ready-for-analysis/)

## Available observations

The OOI metadata export for January 29 through February 3, 2026 inclusive lists **123,609 image regions in 42 moored water samples** from instrument IFCB200 at 7 m. The two samples featured in the replay contain 1,065 and 5,164 regions. The saved gallery contains 12 crops from each. The archive export reports sample volumes and image counts. Expert identifications for these crops remain an open requirement. For comparisons beyond the event, OOI describes two moored acquisition windows spanning roughly 18 months. Its larger station-tagged collection also includes shipboard samples. [Six-day sample metadata (CSV)](https://ifcb-data.oceanobservatories.org/api/export_metadata/PioneerMABMoored?start_date=2026-01-29&end_date=2026-02-03) · [OOI collections and access](https://oceanobservatories.org/2026/09/ooi-plankton-imagery-and-co-located-environmental-data-now-ready-for-analysis/)

## First experiment

We ran the frozen WHOI v2.4 model on the 24 original gallery crops using a local CPU. Its top suggestion was `nanoplankton_mix` for 14 regions and `detritus` for six. Four other labels appeared once each. These unreviewed suggestions establish that the model can read the Pioneer images. The audit below is needed to measure accuracy and estimate composition. The review file in the replay data directory, `pioneer_whoi_predictions.csv`, lists the three highest-scoring suggestions and original image URL for every crop. `pioneer_whoi_run.json` records the exact model revision, software versions, and preprocessing. [Pinned WHOI model](https://huggingface.co/sosiklab/NES-plankton-IFCB-classifier-2022/tree/71bc362e8c75014c27692a8f10637d36f411962e)

1. **Run the existing WHOI classifier.** The Sosik Lab Northeast U.S. Shelf v2.4 model recognizes 155 categories, including diatoms, ciliates, detritus, bubbles, and camera artifacts. The weights are MIT licensed. Its companion dataset contains 97,026 expert-labeled IFCB images under CC BY 4.0. Retain the original class, top three suggestions, model revision, source image ID, and score. A score measures the model's preference among its classes. Local testing can establish how often a prediction with a score near 0.8 is correct at Pioneer. [Model and preprocessing](https://huggingface.co/sosiklab/NES-plankton-IFCB-classifier-2022) · [Training and validation images](https://huggingface.co/datasets/sosiklab/NES-plankton-IFCB-classifier-2022-dataset)
2. **Create a local reference set.** Start with a proposed budget of 10 randomly selected regions per sample (420 images). Have a reviewer experienced with IFCB imagery label them without seeing predictions. Double-review a subset and preserve ambiguous calls. Set aside entire sampling dates for the final test before adjusting the model or thresholds. Add a separate batch of unusual and low-confidence images for improving the model. Keep the random audit independent so its error estimates remain interpretable. Use broad groups when species-level distinctions are unreliable. [WHOI illustrated IFCB guide](https://whoigit.github.io/whoi-plankton/)
3. **Measure the event.** For each sample, divide the number of image regions assigned to each broad group by its analyzed volume in mL. Carry unresolved regions and instrument artifacts as separate counts. Report per-group precision and recall, fraction unresolved, and error in estimated group concentrations against the independent audit. Retain sample size and uncertainty when comparing storm phases. Expand the audit for groups with too few examples. Across pooled samples, sum regions and volumes before dividing. [Quantifying abundance under changing plankton communities](https://doi.org/10.1002/lom3.10399)

## What the language model would do

Give the model a question, a table of code-calculated results with sample IDs and QC flags, selected image examples and reviewer decisions, and retrieved passages. Use OOI for station history and observations, WHOI for image exemplars, WoRMS for accepted names, and primary studies for ecology. Have the response identify **observations, plausible explanations, evidence for each, and the measurement that would distinguish them**. For instance, a salinity drop can motivate a transport hypothesis. Current direction and upstream observations would help test it. The source of the water remains an open question. [WoRMS taxonomic services](https://www.marinespecies.org/aphia.php?p=webservice) · [OOI event observations](https://oceanobservatories.org/2026/09/ooi-plankton-imagery-and-co-located-environmental-data-now-ready-for-analysis/)

Use the OpenAI Responses API to write one cited report per event or comparison. Retrieve sources once and reuse the evidence across models. Score reports on numerical accuracy, whether each citation supports its claim, and whether the model flags missing evidence. Include questions about sampling gaps and a deliberately unanswerable species or causal question. Compare an untuned local Qwen3-8B with OpenAI using identical evidence. If the local model repeatedly misses a reporting requirement, try a small QLoRA adapter using expert-reviewed examples. Profile GPU memory on the L40S with a short batch first. [OpenAI file search](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-file-search) · [OpenAI structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs) · [Qwen3-8B model and license](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-8B) · [QLoRA adapters](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/quantization/finetune)

## Three extensions

| Project | Testable question | First comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Diatom chain length | Did cells per chain change during the storm? | Count cells in clearly visible chains, retain an unreadable category, and compare predictions with independent manual counts. [Original IFCB chain-counting study](https://doi.org/10.1093/plankt/fbaf064) |
| Historical analog search | Which other periods most resemble Gianna in water properties and plankton composition? | Rank historical windows using salinity, pressure, and chlorophyll, then add image-group concentrations. Compare rankings against independently selected events, matching season and sampling coverage. |
| Six-hour community forecast | Can earlier observations improve estimates of diatom or detritus concentration at future sample times? | Across the longer moored record, compare persistence, sensors only, and sensors plus earlier images, holding out whole events or deployments. Score only observed targets. Raw images arrive after recovery, so evaluate image-assisted forecasts as retrospective experiments.
