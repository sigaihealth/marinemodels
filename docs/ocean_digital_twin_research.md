# Open Ocean Data for Multimodal Models and Digital Twins

Research brief · November 24, 2026

Start with **a storm and plankton replay at OOI’s Pioneer Mid-Atlantic Bight Central Surface Mooring**. It combines microscopic imagery, optical measurements, water-column profiles, and weather at a documented event. For a second project with a clear numerical forecast target, build a **near-bottom oxygen model on the Pacific Northwest shelf**. Both projects can grow into site-scale digital twins by updating their estimated state with new observations and testing predictions against data withheld from the updates.

## What OOI makes available

The NSF Ocean Observatories Initiative observes selected ocean sites with buoys, fixed moorings, vertically profiling instruments, gliders, and cabled seafloor stations. Available modalities include meteorology and waves, temperature and salinity, currents, dissolved oxygen and carbonate chemistry, optical fluorescence and backscatter, plankton images, video, active sonar, passive hydrophone audio, seismic records, and shipboard water samples. Coverage varies by site and deployment. Cabled instruments can stream observations, while some moored and mobile records are only complete after recovery. [OOI arrays](https://oceanobservatories.org/research-arrays/) · [Data products](https://oceanobservatories.org/data-products/) · [Data access](https://oceanobservatories.org/data-access/)

Use Data Explorer and ERDDAP to subset numerical data by time and variable in CSV or NetCDF. Use THREDDS Gold Copy for full-resolution deployment data and the Raw Data Archive or IFCB Dashboard for imagery. On OOI JupyterHub, the shared read-only ~/ooi tree includes Raw Data Archive material and processed NetCDF under ~/ooi/kdata. OOI asks users to acknowledge OOI and NSF and cite the platform DOI, instrument or product, date window, repository, and access date. [Data Explorer documentation](https://dataexplorer.oceanobservatories.org/help/overview.html) · [JupyterHub storage guide](https://ooifb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ooi_jupyterhub_access_and_setup-1.pdf) · [Citation guidance](https://oceanobservatories.org/how-to-use-acknowledge-and-cite-data/)

## First notebook for the Pioneer storm replay

1. **Freeze the event and site.** Use CP10CNSM and January 29–February 4, 2026 UTC as a proposed working window around Winter Storm Gianna. OOI reports a rapid pressure drop, about a one-unit drop in practical salinity, a rise in IFCB regions of interest per mL, and a visible change in imaged assemblages. OOI reports about 18 months of recovered moored IFCB data in two windows from April 2024 to April 2026. The station-tagged 7 million-plus image collection includes shipboard samples. For site training use the moored-only collection. [OOI storm analysis](https://oceanobservatories.org/2026/09/ooi-plankton-imagery-and-co-located-environmental-data-now-ready-for-analysis/) · [OOI’s comparison view](https://dataexplorer.oceanobservatories.org/?ls=AFm3fAL0) · [Moored-only IFCB](https://ifcb-data.oceanobservatories.org/timeline?dataset=PioneerMABMoored)
2. **Start with three aligned views.** Plot pressure, wind, salinity and chlorophyll. Place the OOI image mosaics or recovered image bins next to the time series. Show the available vertical profiles alongside, with measured and estimated intervals visible. IFCB raw image regions are released after recovery. Its numerical telemetry includes sampled volume and ROI/mL, a count of image regions that can include particles. Use volume-weighted summaries across bins and treat taxon labels as a separate annotation task. [IFCB raw data and telemetry](https://discourse.oceanobservatories.org/t/quick-access-links-to-ooi-ifcb-plims-images-data-and-metadata/990)
3. **Compare models in order.** Use persistence and a seasonal average first, then sensors only, then sensors plus prior image features, then satellite or current context when present. The replay can use recovered imagery. A live forecast must use only data available at forecast issuance. Use a retrospective image model to test potential value before choosing a live proxy.
4. **Test on held-out deployments.** Group by date and image bin so images from the same water sample stay together. Hold out whole events or deployments. Report coverage, chlorophyll error, event precision and recall, lead time, and uncertainty. Keep UTC observation and availability timestamps, position, actual sensor depth, sample volume, instrument deployment, units, QC flags and annotations in a provenance table. A sensor’s first and last catalog timestamps are an availability envelope. [OOI quality control](https://oceanobservatories.org/quality-control/)
5. **Add a model that updates with measurements.** Estimate a small state such as upper and lower layer temperature and salinity, chlorophyll, and particle anomaly. Propagate using wind and mixing or transport terms, then update as observations arrive. Show uncertainty growing when inputs are missing. A broader 3D field would require gridded background physics and validation at additional locations.

## Small public data requests verified

Use CPU resources for time alignment and baseline models. A GPU can accelerate batched image embeddings or spectrogram models. Start with small site and date subsets and check the storage quota of the execution environment before staging raw imagery.
