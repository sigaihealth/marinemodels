# Weather and satellite data for the Pioneer storm replay

The first experiment asks whether storm mixing or transport of different shelf water better explains a change in the particles seen by the Pioneer microscope. The Central Surface Mooring collected 42 samples at 7 m from January 29 through February 3, 2026 UTC. Use each acquisition as an anchor. The satellite queries below use 35.95° N, 75.12° W, rounded from the replay coordinates. [OOI event report](https://oceanobservatories.org/2026/09/ooi-plankton-imagery-and-co-located-environmental-data-now-ready-for-analysis/) · [Existing replay data and QC](https://github.com/sigaihealth/marinemodels/tree/main/world-model)

## One acquisition across instruments

On February 1 at 15:25:45 UTC, Pioneer collected sample D20260201T152545_IFCB200. At 15:26 the independent Oregon Inlet wave buoy (44095, about 29 km away) reported 5.60 m significant wave height and a 13.33 s dominant period. At 15:30 the OOI Southern Surface Mooring (41083, about 35 km away) recorded wind at 16.4 m/s from 348° true. The time printed on each buoy record is its reporting timestamp. The Southern buoy's record on NDBC is a second distribution channel for the OOI instrument. Count it once. [Microscope sample](https://ifcb-data.oceanobservatories.org/bin?dataset=PioneerMABMoored&bin=D20260201T152545_IFCB200) · [Oregon Inlet wave records](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/view_text_file.php?filename=4409522026.txt.gz&dir=data/stdmet/Feb/) · [Southern mooring weather records](https://www.ndbc.noaa.gov/view_text_file.php?filename=4108322026.txt.gz&dir=data/stdmet/Feb/)

## First model comparison

Use one row per microscope acquisition, giving 42 rows for this event. Keep the original UTC time, measurement time, station or pixel location, horizontal separation, depth, averaging interval, units, source version and quality flags. Keep a satellite coverage mask. For the gridded products, also retain a small shelf map so a moving temperature or color front can be distinguished from a change at one pixel.

Start with the most recent mooring temperature and salinity as a persistence forecast. Compare that with models using mooring history, then add winds and waves, then satellite temperature gradients and currents. Score 6-, 24- and 72-hour forecasts on whole storms held out from training, using inputs available at each forecast issue time. Treat the six days here as one replay. Evaluate plankton or other particle categories separately using reviewed labels and analyzed sample volume.
